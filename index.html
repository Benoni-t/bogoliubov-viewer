Phase, bandwidth and power conventions

The probe coordinate is signed (fpr − fpump) and the physical idler is at fpump − (fpr − fpump). With the physical-output reference, the probe phase is pump-on over pump-off and the idler phase uses a real unit input probe. Remove the pump phase to reproduce the original pump-frame phase convention. This selection applies the same phase offset in every view. Lossless idler curves lift by π at conversion zeros and break at each zero. At exactly zero detuning the displayed result is the coincident-sideband limit.

Chip power is mapped to Ipk with a 50 Ω reference. The displayed model ceiling combines Ipk ≤ 0.3 Ic and a healing-band Taylor accuracy test against the exact circuit. Beyond that ceiling, computed data are formal. Above Ipk = Ic the maps leave the requested range empty. The vertical map guides mark the inlet and outlet healing scales. The Taylor frequency band is reported separately.

The dispersion view marks the available pump frequencies on the bare circuit curve. The selected pump is highlighted there and at zero detuning in the local Bogoliubov branches. The bare curve uses the full circuit expression up to 20 MHz below the plasma frequency. The branches use the selected dispersion order and the indicated chip power and location.

Amplitudes use the same complex solutions as the phases. Probe and idler amplitude are in dB relative to the incident probe. Grey areas or breaks mark excluded data or phases below the amplitude floor. Both maps use the identical 401 × 401 calculation grid. The lossy phase is seeded by a dense frequency sweep at the lowest power and lifted along the power axis. Gaps mark links where the frequency and power continuations disagree or where the phase becomes unresolved. The 3D surfaces interpolate between displayed samples. The line cuts show each lossless conversion zero as a break. Run 2 supplies the two conditional dispersion fits. Loss tangent is imposed.
